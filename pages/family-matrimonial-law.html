Divorce & Separation

What is the difference between a Mutual Consent Divorce and a Contested Divorce?

In a Mutual Consent Divorce, both spouses agree to end the marriage amicably and jointly decide on terms such as alimony, child custody, and property division. It is faster, less expensive, and less emotionally taxing. In a Contested Divorce, one spouse files for divorce without the other’s agreement on specific legal grounds such as cruelty, desertion, or adultery, and the court decides the outcome after hearing evidence from both sides.

How long does a Mutual Consent Divorce take in India?

Generally, a mutual consent divorce takes between 6 to 18 months. Under the law, there is a mandatory 6-month cooling-off period between the First Motion and Second Motion hearings. However, following Supreme Court guidelines, courts can waive this 6-month waiting period if both spouses have already lived separately for over a year and all settlement terms are fully resolved.

Can I file for divorce immediately after marriage?

Under most Indian matrimonial laws, a couple must wait one year from the date of marriage before filing a divorce petition. In exceptional cases involving extreme hardship or exceptional depravity, a party may apply to the court for special permission to file earlier.

Is mediation mandatory before fighting a divorce case in court?

Yes, Family Courts routinely refer couples to court-appointed counselors or mediators before proceeding with a contested trial. The goal is to explore whether reconciliation is possible or if the couple can reach an amicable settlement on divorce terms outside the courtroom.

Alimony, Maintenance & Property

How is the amount of alimony or monthly maintenance decided by the court?

There is no fixed formula. Courts consider several factors, including the income and assets of both spouses, the standard of living maintained during the marriage, the financial needs of the dependent spouse and children, and the duration of the marriage. Both spouses are legally required to file an affidavit disclosing their complete income, assets, and liabilities.

What is Stridhan, and can a wife reclaim it during separation?

Stridhan includes all gifts, jewelry, cash, property, and valuables given to a woman before, during, or after her marriage by her parents, relatives, husband, or in-laws. Legally, Stridhan belongs exclusively to the woman, and she has the absolute right to reclaim it at any time, even during or after separation.

Can a working wife still claim maintenance?

Yes. Earning an income does not automatically disqualify a wife from claiming maintenance. If her income is significantly lower than her husband’s and is insufficient to maintain the standard of living she was accustomed to in the matrimonial home, the court may still grant maintenance.

Child Custody & Guardianship

Who gets custody of the child after a divorce?

Indian courts decide custody based strictly on one principle: the welfare and best interests of the child. While custody of children under 5 years of age is generally granted to the mother, courts evaluate emotional bonding, stability, financial capability, and the child’s own preference (if old enough to express one) before deciding sole or joint custody.

If one parent gets physical custody, what rights does the other parent have?

The non-custodial parent is almost always granted visitation rights, which may include weekend visits, holiday sharing, video calls, and participation in important decisions regarding the child’s education and health.
