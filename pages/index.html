Frequently asked questions

General answers only. Your case may differ, so please contact us to confirm.

What is the difference between a Mutual Consent Divorce and a Contested Divorce?

In a Mutual Consent Divorce, both spouses agree to end the marriage amicably and jointly decide on terms such as alimony, child custody, and property division. It is faster, less expensive, and less emotionally taxing. In a Contested Divorce, one spouse files for divorce without the other’s agreement on specific legal grounds such as cruelty, desertion, or adultery, and the court decides the outcome after hearing evidence from both sides.

Who can have a court marriage?

Under the Special Marriage Act, 1954, two eligible adults may marry by civil ceremony irrespective of religion. We check eligibility for your case before anything is filed.

I live abroad. Do I have to travel to India for every court hearing?

Not necessarily. In many stages of matrimonial proceedings—especially mutual consent divorces and interim hearings—courts allow representation through a Special Power of Attorney (SPA) holder (such as a parent or trusted relative) and permit appearances via video conferencing, subject to the court’s approval.
