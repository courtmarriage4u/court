**Court Marriage vs Traditional Marriage Registration: What Bihar Law Actually Requires**

At CourtMarriage4U one question walks through our door every now and then : "Is court marriage the same as registering our marriage?"

It's not — and getting this wrong costs couples weeks of avoidable delay.

Here's the difference, in plain bulleted terms:

★     **Court Marriage (Special Marriage Act, 1954)**

\- For couples marrying without religious rites, or from different religions

\- Requires a 30-day public notice before the marriage is even solemnized

\- 30-day residency requirement in the district of court marriage

\- Minimum age requirement : 21 (groom), 18 (bride)

\- The certificate you get is your legal marriage — nothing else needed after              

 - Other important conditions include Monogamy, Mental Capacity, Prohibited  degrees of relationship (unless allowed by local customs).

★     **Traditional Marriage + Registration (Bihar Marriage Registration Rules, 2006)**

  - This one is for couples already married or solemnized through religious/customary rites 

 - Must be registered within 30 days of the ceremony —allowed later but with penalty 

 - Public notice period not required, since the marriage already happened 

 - Filed with your local Marriage Registrar ( Mukhia/Ward Councillor) 

 - Important documents needed include proof the wedding took place ( invite card,  photos, priest's certificate , joint photograph of the couple), Proof of date of birth for bride and groom, Address and residence proof (Voter ID, Aadhaar, Passport), Identity proof of witnesses

The one-line way we explain it to clients:

Court marriage creates the marriage. | Registration only records one that already happened.

Both give you a certificate valid for passports, banks, and property.

The mistake isn't in picking "the wrong one" — it's not knowing which process meets your requirements and losing a month figuring it out the hard way.

We've handled both routes for clients across Patna, and the paperwork details are where most delays happen — not the law itself.
